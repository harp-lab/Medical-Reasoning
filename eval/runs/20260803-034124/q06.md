## Answer

## Answer

**Yes — there is exactly one other drug, and yes, they overlap on their two core indications.**

### The other drug: Zonisamide (`DRUGBANK:DB00909`)

Searching KG2 for drugs that *decrease/downregulate* all three targets — CA2, SCN5A, and SCN1A — returned three CURIEs, but two of them (`DRUGBANK:DB00273`, `DrugCentral:2706`) are just Topiramate itself under different vocabularies. The only genuinely distinct hit is **Zonisamide**.

This is mechanistically sensible: like Topiramate, Zonisamide is an anticonvulsant that both **inhibits carbonic anhydrase** (CA2) and **blocks voltage-gated sodium channels** (SCN1A/SCN5A) — the same dual mechanism is why they came out together.

> Note on the query: KG2 encodes "downregulation" as `edge_dir` direction `"decreased"` (via `biolink:regulates`/`biolink:affects`), not the literal string `"downregulated"`. The first pass returned zero rows until I corrected for that.

### Do they treat the same diseases?

**Two indications are shared** (matched directly and through equivalent-CURIE joins):

| Shared disease | 
|---|
| **Epilepsy** |
| **Alcohol dependence** (chronic alcoholic intoxication) |

The rest of each drug's `treats` profile diverges:

| Topiramate-only | Zonisamide-only |
|---|---|
| Simple partial seizures, tonic-clonic epilepsy, Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, essential tremor | Alcohol Use Disorder |
| Obesity, metabolic syndrome, hyperlipidemia, hypertriglyceridemia | Lewy Body Disease |
| | Bipolar disorder |

**Summary:** Zonisamide is the only other drug in KG2 that downregulates CA2 + SCN5A + SCN1A. The two share their central indications — **epilepsy** and **alcohol dependence** — consistent with their shared carbonic-anhydrase + sodium-channel mechanism. They then branch: Topiramate carries a broad seizure/metabolic (weight, lipids) profile, while Zonisamide extends into movement/neuropsychiatric territory (Lewy body disease, bipolar disorder).

One caveat: these `treats` edges are KG2's aggregated assertions (spanning approved, off-label, and literature-derived uses), so a couple — e.g. Topiramate for hyperlipidemia — reflect metabolic side-effect literature rather than formal indications.

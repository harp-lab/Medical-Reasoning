## Answer

**Genes related to Parkinson's or Alzheimer's disease**

Querying RTX-KG2 for `gene_associated_with_condition` edges into Parkinson disease (MONDO:0005180) and Alzheimer disease (MONDO:0004975) returns **3,874 gene–disease associations** — far too many to list, and many genes are linked to *both* diseases. Familiar disease genes show up in the set, e.g. **LRRK2, SNCAIP, PINK1‑AS, HTRA2, DRD2, APOE (ApoE4), MAPT (tau), SOD1, BDNF, TNF, GBA-adjacent lysosomal genes**, alongside thousands of more peripheral associations from text-mined and curated sources.

**Which of these induce tremor?**

Intersecting that PD/AD gene set with genes that link to *tremor* (HP:0001337, joined through `equiv` across vocabularies) yields just **two genes**, and notably each is associated with **both** AD and PD:

| Gene | Assoc. diseases | Link to tremor |
|------|-----------------|----------------|
| **HTR1A** (serotonin 5‑HT1A receptor) | AD + PD | `biolink:causes` Tremor |
| **STUB1** (CHIP, E3 ubiquitin ligase) | AD + PD | `biolink:causes` Tremor |

Both connect to tremor via a `biolink:causes` edge (HTR1A also has a `physically_interacts_with` edge to tremor).

---

**Caveats on interpretation:**
- I read "induce tremor" as a gene→tremor edge of type `causes`, `contributes_to`, or `gene_associated_with_condition`. Only `causes` edges actually matched. Broadening to weaker predicates (`affects`, `related_to`, `correlated_with`) would enlarge the list.
- "Related to a disease" here means the single `gene_associated_with_condition` predicate. If you also want causal/variant links (`disease_has_basis_in`, `is_sequence_variant_of`, `biomarker_for`), the base gene set — and possibly the tremor intersection — would grow. Want me to widen either side?

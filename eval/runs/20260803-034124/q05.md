## Answer

## Answer

Only **one** drug in RTX-KG2 satisfies both conditions: **topiramate** (`DrugCentral:2706`, listed under the brand name "topina").

**How it matches:**
- **Upregulates GABRB2** (a GABA-A receptor β2 subunit) — via a `regulates`/`increased` edge.
- **Downregulates two sodium channel genes** — `regulates`/`decreased` edges to **SCN1A** (`UniProtKB:P35498`) and **SCN5A** (`UniProtKB:Q14524`). (It also downregulates the carbonic anhydrases CA2 and CA4, which is consistent with topiramate's known pharmacology.)

Four other drugs *increase* GABRB2 — **etomidate** (amidate), **zopiclone** (imovane), **methoxyflurane**, and **trichloroethyl phosphate** — but none of them have a *decreases* edge to a sodium channel gene in the graph, so they don't qualify.

**What topiramate treats** (per `biolink:treats` edges):

| Condition |
|---|
| Lennox-Gastaut syndrome |
| Simple partial seizures |
| Epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures |
| Essential tremor |
| Chronic alcoholism (alcohol dependence) |

This fits its mechanism nicely — topiramate is an anticonvulsant that both **potentiates GABA-A inhibition** (↑GABRB2) and **blocks voltage-gated sodium channels** (↓SCN1A/SCN5A), which is exactly why it's used across these seizure disorders.

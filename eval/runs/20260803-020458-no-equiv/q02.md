## Answer

**Genes related to Parkinson's or Alzheimer's disease**

Querying KG2 for `gene_associated_with_condition` edges into Parkinson disease (MONDO:0005180) and Alzheimer disease (MONDO:0004975) returns a large set — **3,874 gene–disease association edges** (hundreds of distinct genes per disease, the familiar PD/AD panels: SNCA, LRRK2, PARK7, PINK1, APP, PSEN1/2, APOE, MAPT, and many more). That's too many to list usefully, so the interesting part is the second half of your question.

**Which of those genes induce tremor**

I intersected the disease-gene set with genes that link to the *tremor* phenotype (HP:0001337) through a causal-type predicate (`causes`, `predisposes`, `contributes_to`, `affects`). One wrinkle worth noting: the disease-association edges are keyed on `HGNC:` ids while the tremor edges are keyed on `NCBIGene:` ids for the same genes, so I bridged the two namespaces via `same_as`/`close_match` before matching. **Six genes** survive:

| Gene | Associated with | Relationship to tremor |
|------|-----------------|------------------------|
| **APP** | Alzheimer's + Parkinson's | predisposes |
| **HTR1A** (serotonin receptor 1A) | Alzheimer's + Parkinson's | causes |
| **STUB1** | Alzheimer's + Parkinson's | causes |
| **VIM** (vimentin) | Alzheimer's + Parkinson's | predisposes |
| **SGCE** (ε-sarcoglycan) | Parkinson's | affects |
| **EEF1A2** | Parkinson's | affects |

**Caveats**
- "Induce" I read as a causal/predisposing link to the tremor phenotype node. If you'd rather include weaker relationships (`related_to`, `coexists_with`) or the many `physically_interacts_with` protein-level edges, the set would grow — say the word and I'll rerun with a broader predicate list.
- KG2 aggregates from many sources (including text-mined assertions like SemMedDB), so a `causes` edge is an asserted relationship in the literature, not a curated clinical fact. STUB1 and SGCE are strongly tremor/movement-disorder genes; HTR1A, VIM, and EEF1A2 edges are more likely literature-derived and worth spot-checking.

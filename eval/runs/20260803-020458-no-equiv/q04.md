## Answer

Working through KG2, exactly **one drug** in the graph both downregulates CA2 and upregulates GABRB2:

### 💊 Topiramate (DrugCentral:2706 · DrugBank DB00273 · ATC N03AX11)

The knowledge graph carries both regulation edges with matching direction qualifiers, so the mechanism lines up cleanly with the patient's dysregulation:

| Edge | Target | Direction | Effect on patient |
|------|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| `biolink:regulates` | CA2 (UniProtKB:P00918) | **decreased** | counters the CA2 overexpression |
| `biolink:regulates` | GABRB2 (UniProtKB:P47870) | **increased** | counters the GABRB2 underexpression |

This is consistent with topiramate's known pharmacology — it's a carbonic-anhydrase inhibitor (CA2) and a positive modulator of GABA-A receptors (of which GABRB2 is a subunit).

### 🩺 Diseases topiramate is known to treat (per KG2 `biolink:treats`)

- **Epilepsy with generalized tonic-clonic seizures** (DOID:7725)
- **Simple partial seizures** (UMLS:C0234974)
- **Lennox-Gastaut syndrome** (UMLS:C0238111)
- **Essential tremor** (DOID:4990)
- **Chronic alcohol intoxication / alcohol use disorder** (UMLS:C0001973)

---

**Notes on scope / caveats:**
- I restricted the genes to the **human** orthologs (CA2 = NCBIGene:760, GABRB2 = NCBIGene:2561). The top NameRes hits were non-human orthologs not present in KG2, so I used the human cliques — appropriate for a patient question.
- "Downregulate" = direction `decreased`/`downregulated`; "upregulate" = `increased`/`upregulated`, across any predicate carrying a direction qualifier. In practice only `biolink:regulates` produced the matches.
- This is a single knowledge-graph's assertions, not clinical advice. KG2 also lists a set of **contraindications** for topiramate (e.g., glaucoma, nephrolithiasis, pregnancy, hepatic/renal impairment) that would matter before any real-world use.
